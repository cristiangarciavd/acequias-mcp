# Grape varietals of Mendoza - fact card and research notes

The eight varietals of the game's roulette. The **fact card** below is the
player-facing part, for the rules sheet ([`rules-v3.md`](rules-v3.md)) and the printed
roulette: each fact comes from an Argentine official or institutional source (the
Instituto Nacional de Vitivinicultura, INV; the Universidad Nacional de Cuyo; Bodegas
de Argentina), linked next to it. The **research notes** after it are the longer
background, with planted-area figures read from the INV tables and some secondary
sources; use them for context, not for print.

## Fact card

### Malbec - violet, 6

- Came to Mendoza with the French vines of the *Quinta Agronómica*, run by the French
  agronomist Michel Aimé Pouget, whom Sarmiento hired. The project for the school was
  presented to Mendoza's legislature on **17 April 1853** ([UNCuyo][uncuyo-malbec];
  [Bodegas de Argentina][bda-malbec]).
- That date is **Malbec World Day**, chosen by Wines of Argentina in 2011
  ([UNCuyo][uncuyo-malbec]; [Bodegas de Argentina][bda-malbec]).
- Argentina's most planted grape: 47,064 ha, about **one vine in four** in the
  country; Mendoza grows 39,856 ha of it, about 85% ([INV 2024][inv-sup]).
- Its biggest block is the Valle de Uco; Luján de Cuyo is the top single department
  ([INV 2024][inv-sup]).

### Syrah - violet, 6

- Grown across Europe and best known from France's Rhône; INV notes that its origin
  is uncertain, with tradition linking it to Shiraz in ancient Persia
  ([INV Syrah][inv-syrah]; [INV Syrah 2024 report][inv-syrah-pdf]).
- Expressive wines with blackberry, plum, cherry and leather, plus spice and herbs
  such as thyme and rosemary ([INV Syrah][inv-syrah]).
- 10,420 ha in Argentina and 7,700 in Mendoza, with San Juan second; in Mendoza it
  has its largest share in the warm East, around San Martín ([INV 2024][inv-sup]).

### Bonarda - violet, 9 (rare)

- Comes from Savoy, in France next to Italy; the French call it Corbeau, and in the
  United States it is Charbono ([INV Bonarda][inv-bonarda]).
- Arrived in Argentina with the immigrants of the late 19th century
  ([INV Bonarda][inv-bonarda]).
- Argentina's second red: 16,774 ha, 14,027 of them in Mendoza (about 84%), mostly in
  the East: San Martín, Rivadavia, Santa Rosa, Junín ([INV 2024][inv-sup]).
- Long a blending grape for its big yields and deep color; with lower yields it now
  makes fine single-varietal wines ([INV Bonarda][inv-bonarda]).

### Cabernet Sauvignon - red, 6

- From Bordeaux, France: a natural cross of Cabernet Franc and Sauvignon Blanc
  ([INV Cabernet Sauvignon][inv-cs]).
- Its berries have thick, very dark skins rich in tannins
  ([INV Cabernet Sauvignon][inv-cs]).
- Argentina's #4 grape with 12,727 ha (9,787 in Mendoza), but shrinking: 2,832 ha
  fewer than in 2015 ([INV 2024][inv-sup]).
- In Mendoza its largest blocks are in the Valle de Uco and Luján de Cuyo
  ([INV 2024][inv-sup]).

### Merlot - red, 6

- Probably from south-west France; its name may come from the blackbird (*merle*)
  ([INV Merlot][inv-merlot]).
- Full-bodied, balanced wines with elegant tannins: plum and sour cherry, rose,
  spice, black pepper and cinnamon ([INV Merlot][inv-merlot]).
- 4,348 ha in Argentina and 3,449 in Mendoza; the main block is the Valle de Uco
  (Tupungato, Tunuyán) ([INV 2024][inv-sup]).

### Cabernet Franc - red, 9 (rare)

- Native to Bordeaux and widely grown in the Loire. Argentina had only 76 ha up to
  1990; the planting boom came after 2000 ([INV Cabernet Franc][inv-cf]).
- Ruby red, with green bell pepper, eucalyptus, menthol, spice and red berries;
  elegant and structured ([INV Cabernet Franc][inv-cf]).
- 2,009 ha in Argentina and 1,661 in Mendoza, more than double the 2015 area; over
  half of Mendoza's grows in the Valle de Uco ([INV 2024][inv-sup]).
- Its day is **4 December** ([INV Cabernet Franc][inv-cf]).

### Torrontés - fluorescent green, 6

- Argentina's own grape: Torrontés Riojano and Torrontés Sanjuanino are crosses of
  Muscat of Alexandria and Criolla Chica; the third type is Torrontés Mendocino
  ([INV Torrontés][inv-torrontes]; [Agüero et al. 2003][aguero]).
- Aromatic, yet it makes a **dry** wine with a distinctive Muscat flavor
  ([INV Torrontés][inv-torrontes]).
- Torrontés Riojano covers 6,867 ha; Mendoza grows 3,207 ha of it, the most of any
  province, mostly in the East and Lavalle ([INV 2024][inv-sup]).

### Chardonnay - fluorescent green, 6

- France's main white grape, grown in Burgundy, Chablis and Champagne
  ([INV Chardonnay][inv-chardonnay]).
- In Argentina it is the base of most sparkling wines
  ([INV Chardonnay][inv-chardonnay]).
- 5,542 ha in Argentina and 4,606 in Mendoza (about 83%); Tupungato, in the Valle de
  Uco, is the top department ([INV 2024][inv-sup]).
- Versatile and aromatically complex, with good ageing potential
  ([INV Chardonnay][inv-chardonnay]).

## Research notes

Background for the eight varietals in the game. Planted-area figures come from the
**INV Informe Anual de Superficie 2024** (vineyard registry as of 31 Dec 2024), read
straight from the PDF tables. Where possible they were cross-checked against INV
single-variety reports and press summaries. Argentina had about 201,000 ha of vines in
2024, and Mendoza had 142,785 ha (about 71%). The INV single-variety reports sometimes
give other totals for the same year (for example 13,006 ha of Cabernet Sauvignon and
10,658 ha of Syrah in 2024), because they read the registry at another date; the fact
card uses the annual report's figures throughout. Facts from non-Argentine or press
sources (Decanter, Jancis Robinson, Wine Spectator, Wine Folly, Wikipedia, Forbes) are
background only and are not used on the fact card.

Zone mapping used below. The department tables were added up into the zones the game uses:

- **Valle de Uco**: Tupungato, Tunuyán, San Carlos
- **Luján de Cuyo**, **Maipú**: one department each
- **San Rafael**: the South oasis; General Alvear is listed apart
- **Este** (East): San Martín, Rivadavia, Junín, Santa Rosa, La Paz
- **Lavalle**: the North oasis, which falls outside the five game zones

Items marked **(uncertain)** have weak or conflicting sources.

## Malbec

**Key facts**
- **Origin:** Malbec comes from south-west France (Cahors, where it is called Côt). The French agronomist Michel Aimé Pouget brought it to Mendoza. Sarmiento had hired him to run the Quinta Normal agricultural school, which the provincial government approved on 17 April 1853. Some sources say 1868 for the arrival **(uncertain; 1853 is the better-documented date)**.
- **Area:** Malbec is #1 in Argentina with 47,064 ha in 2024, or 23.5% of all vines. Mendoza holds 39,856 ha of it, about 85% of the national total and 27.9% of Mendoza's vineyard. It grew by +7,578 ha nationally in 2015-2024 (INV 2024).
- **Zones:** Valle de Uco has about 16,580 ha, the largest block. Luján de Cuyo is the top single department with 8,920 ha. Then come Este (about 6,680), Maipú (4,070) and San Rafael (2,190).
- **Style:** Deep purple wine with plum, black cherry, violet and mocha aromas. Tannins are plush. Wines from high Uco vineyards are fresher and more floral.

**Useful**
- Malbec is the classic match for an Argentine *asado* (bife de chorizo, ojo de bife). Serve it at about 16-18 °C.

**Curious**
- Malbec World Day falls on **17 April**, the 1853 school date. Wines of Argentina created it in 2011.
- Vine-pull schemes in the 1980s ripped out much of Argentina's Malbec. Its comeback since the 1990s made it the country's flagship grape.

**Game blurb:** *Argentina's flagship red, brought from France in 1853; one vine in four in the country is Malbec.*

## Cabernet Sauvignon

**Key facts**
- **Origin:** Cabernet Sauvignon is a Bordeaux variety. It reached Argentina around the 1850s with the wave of French cuttings, many of which came via Chile **(the date is approximate)**.
- **Area:** It is #4 in Argentina with 12,727 ha in 2024. Mendoza holds 9,787 ha, or 6.9% of its vineyard. The grape is shrinking: -2,832 ha nationally and -2,112 ha in Mendoza over 2015-2024 (INV).
- **Zones:** Valle de Uco has about 2,975 ha (about 30% of Mendoza's Cabernet). Luján de Cuyo has 1,957 ha (about 20%), the traditional heartland (Agrelo, Perdriel). Maipú has 1,191 ha and San Rafael 1,036.
- **Style:** Cassis, blackberry, red pepper and tobacco. Tannins are firm and the wines age well. Argentine versions are riper and softer than Bordeaux.

**Useful**
- Pair it with slow-roasted beef or hard aged cheeses. Decant young bottles for about an hour.

**Curious**
- Laura Catena says that before Malbec took over, Cabernet was Argentina's most prestigious red grape.
- Much Argentine Cabernet grows from ungrafted, pre-phylloxera massal selections rather than modern clones.

**Game blurb:** *Bordeaux's noble red; before Malbec's rise it was Argentina's prestige grape. Firm, cassis-scented, long-lived.*

## Cabernet Franc

**Key facts**
- **Origin:** Cabernet Franc comes from Bordeaux and the Loire. For decades Argentina grew only a few hundred hectares, mostly as a blending grape. Serious single-varietal planting began in the 1990s and 2000s, notably Catena's high-altitude plots in Gualtallary at about 1,500 m.
- **Area:** It is #17 in Argentina with 2,009 ha in 2024, and Mendoza has 1,661 ha. It is the fastest-growing fine red: +1,173 ha since 2015, more than double. It gained +151 ha in 2024 alone, the biggest one-year rise of any top-25 variety (INV 2024).
- **Zones:** Valle de Uco has about 944 ha, 57% of Mendoza's Cabernet Franc and about 47% of Argentina's (Tunuyán 450, Tupungato 272, San Carlos 222). It also grows in Luján de Cuyo (411 ha) and Maipú (119 ha).
- **Style:** Red fruit, raspberry, graphite, and a herbal or peppery edge (a hint of green bell pepper). It is mid-weight and fresh.

**Useful**
- Good with lamb, herb-crusted meats, or roasted vegetables. It shows best slightly cooler than other reds, at about 15-16 °C.

**Curious**
- Cabernet Franc is a parent of both Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot.
- Wine Spectator has called it Argentina's "secret weapon". Several top-scoring Argentine reds of recent years are Uco Cabernet Franc.

**Game blurb:** *The rising star: Argentina's plantings more than doubled in a decade, led by high Uco vineyards.*

## Syrah

**Key facts**
- **Origin:** Syrah comes from the northern Rhône (France). Old legends place its origin in Shiraz, Persia, and the INV report repeats this, but DNA shows a French origin. It is found in almost every Argentine wine province.
- **Area:** It is #6 in Argentina with 10,420 ha in 2024, or 5.2% of all vines. Mendoza holds 7,700 ha (73.9%) and San Juan 1,930 ha (18.5%). Area fell by 19% over 2015-2024 (INV Syrah report, Feb 2025).
- **Zones:** Mostly in the warm plains. Este has about 3,360 ha (San Martín alone has 1,503), Lavalle in the North has 1,450, and San Rafael has 1,052. Smaller plots grow in Valle de Uco and Maipú.
- **Style:** INV describes blackberry, plum, cherry and leather, with spice and herbs such as thyme and rosemary. Warm sites give jammy, round wines, while cooler Uco sites give peppery, tighter ones.

**Useful**
- Pair it with spicy grilled meats, *chivito* (kid goat) or barbecue ribs.

**Curious**
- **San Juan**, Mendoza's northern neighbour, built its wine image on Syrah. Even so, Mendoza grows four times more.
- "Syrah" and "Shiraz" are the same grape. Argentina uses the French name.

**Game blurb:** *Spicy Rhône red that loves the hot eastern plains; Mendoza grows three-quarters of Argentina's Syrah.*

## Merlot

**Key facts**
- **Origin:** Merlot is a Bordeaux variety that arrived with the 19th-century French imports **(the exact date is not documented in the sources found)**. It is used mostly in blends.
- **Area:** It is #13 in Argentina with 4,348 ha in 2024, and Mendoza has 3,449 ha. It is declining: Forbes Argentina reports a drop of more than 26% in a decade **(press figure, not cross-checked)**.
- **Zones:** Valle de Uco is the main block with about 1,230 ha (Tupungato 540, Tunuyán 510). Este has about 885 ha, San Rafael 379 and Luján de Cuyo 371. Outside Mendoza, Patagonia (Neuquén, Río Negro) is noted for it.
- **Style:** Soft and round, with plum, black cherry and chocolate. Cooler, high sites (Uco, Patagonia) add structure and freshness.

**Useful**
- An easy food wine: pasta with meat sauce, roast chicken, pork. Serve at about 16 °C.

**Curious**
- Merlot is a child of Cabernet Franc.
- Forbes calls it "the Merlot paradox": it is losing ground in Argentina, yet it makes some of the world's most expensive wines (Pétrus).

**Game blurb:** *Bordeaux's soft, plummy red; a quiet blending partner that shines in cool Uco and Patagonia.*

## Bonarda

**Key facts**
- **Origin:** Argentine Bonarda is **not** the Italian Bonarda. DNA studies (1999-2008) identified it as **Douce Noir** from Savoie in France, the same grape as California's Charbono. It most likely arrived with 19th-century Italian immigrants under the wrong name. In 2011 it was given the name "Bonarda Argentina".
- **Area:** It is #3 in Argentina overall and the #2 red, with 16,774 ha in 2024. Mendoza holds 14,027 ha, about 84% of the national total and 9.8% of Mendoza's vineyard (INV 2024).
- **Zones:** A grape of the warm East. Este has about 7,420 ha (San Martín 3,511, Rivadavia 1,699, Santa Rosa 1,168, Junín 1,031). Lavalle has 2,016 ha and San Rafael 1,564. Very little grows in Luján de Cuyo or Uco.
- **Style:** Juicy, fruity and low in tannin, with fresh plum, cherry and violet. It is often made without oak.

**Useful**
- An everyday wine for pizza, pasta, empanadas or *choripán*. It is nice slightly chilled, at about 14-16 °C.

**Curious**
- Bonarda was long Argentina's most planted red, until Malbec overtook it around 2000 **(uncertain: the date is a widely repeated claim, not from INV)**.
- Its name points to Italy and its DNA to the French Alps, so the grape has a mistaken identity.

**Game blurb:** *A juicy East-side red with a mistaken identity: not Italian at all, but France's Douce Noir.*

## Torrontés

**Key facts**
- **Origin:** Torrontés is Argentina's own white grape. The 2003 DNA study by Agüero et al. (AJEV) showed that Torrontés Riojano is a natural cross of **Muscat of Alexandria × Criolla Chica** (= Listán Prieto / Mission), born in colonial South American vineyards. Two other grapes, Torrontés Sanjuanino and Torrontés Mendocino, are separate varieties.
- **Area:** Torrontés Riojano is #8 in Argentina with 6,867 ha in 2024. Mendoza grows 3,207 ha of it, the most of any province, ahead of San Juan (777), La Rioja (1,680) and Salta (842). Counting all three types, Mendoza has about 3,440 ha, roughly 39% of national Torrontés (INV 2024).
- **Zones:** In Mendoza it is mostly a grape of the East and North: San Martín 847, Lavalle 593, Rivadavia 561 and Santa Rosa 255. Small plots grow in San Rafael and Maipú. Its most famous wines come from Cafayate (Salta), not Mendoza.
- **Style:** Very aromatic: rose, orange blossom, peach and citrus. On the palate it is usually dry, with a light bitter finish.

**Useful**
- Serve cold, at 8-10 °C. It pairs with *empanadas salteñas*, ceviche, and spicy Asian or Indian food.

**Curious**
- It smells sweet like Muscat but is usually bone-dry. Blind tasters are often surprised.
- It is the only top white variety that was born in Argentina, from a Spanish mission grape crossed with a Mediterranean Muscat.

**Game blurb:** *Argentina's own white, born from Muscat and a mission grape; smells sweet, tastes dry.*

## Chardonnay

**Key facts**
- **Origin:** Chardonnay comes from Burgundy. It became important in Mendoza from 1959, when Moët & Chandon chose Agrelo (Luján de Cuyo) for its first winery outside France and needed base wine for sparkling.
- **Area:** It is #9 in Argentina with 5,542 ha in 2024, the top fine white after Torrontés. Mendoza holds 4,606 ha, about 83%. In 2024 it was one of only four top-25 varieties that grew (+18 ha) (INV 2024).
- **Zones:** Valle de Uco has about 1,975 ha, 43% of Mendoza's Chardonnay, and Tupungato alone has 1,167, the top department. The rest grows in Este (about 1,150), Luján de Cuyo (514), Maipú (444) and San Rafael (272).
- **Style:** Uco versions are taut, with citrus, green apple and white flowers, and sometimes a mineral, saline edge. Oaked styles add butter, vanilla and tropical fruit.

**Useful**
- Serve at about 10-12 °C. Pair it with trout from the Andes, chicken in cream sauce, or *provoleta*.

**Curious**
- Catena's Adrianna Vineyard, planted in 1992 in Gualtallary (Tupungato) at about 1,500 m, was the first commercial vineyard in that part of Uco. Its Chardonnays are among the most praised white wines in South America.
- Much of Mendoza's Chardonnay goes into sparkling wine, which is Chandon's reason for coming to Mendoza.

**Game blurb:** *Burgundy's white goes high: crisp Chardonnay from Tupungato, and the base of Mendoza's sparkling wine.*
